\begin{abstract}
We apply the Sabag Bounded Transformation Principle to drug target discovery, predicting DHPS (Deoxyhypusine Synthase) as a novel cancer drug target. Original analysis scored DHPS as maximally bounded ($B = 2$). \textbf{Erratum (November 2026):} re-scoring from curated Reactome reactions gives $B = 4$ for DHPS (tied with SHMT2), so boundedness does not single out DHPS. What the public data do support is the absence of a backup pathway (linear hypusine pathway, no paralog), broad CRISPR dependency (89\% of 1,258 DepMap lines), and zero clinical candidates. The predicted $10.5\times$ tumor selectivity is not supported: DHPS is not upregulated in TCGA lung tumors relative to normal tissue.
\end{abstract}

\section{Bounded Drug Discovery}

\begin{definition}[Boundedness Score]
For a metabolic enzyme target:
$$B = |\text{substrates}| + |\text{products}| + |\text{backup pathways}|$$
Lower scores indicate more constrained (bounded) targets.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Optimal Target Selection]
\emph{Original statement:} targets with $B = 2$ (one substrate, one product, zero backups) provide maximum therapeutic selectivity. \emph{Status:} not supported; across 126 diseases, targets with $B=2$ had existing drugs in 27\% of cases versus 56\% at $B=3$, so $B$ does not rank targets. The decisive variable in the data is the absence of a redundant route.
\end{theorem}

\section{Why DHPS Is Optimal}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Unique substrate}: eIF5A is the ONLY protein that undergoes hypusination
\item \textbf{No backup}: Unlike SHMT2 (which has SHMT1 backup), DHPS has no alternative enzyme (supported: DepMap essential in 89\% of lines versus 2\% for SHMT2)
\item \textbf{MYC dependency}: MYC-driven cancers require hypusinated eIF5A for translation of polyproline-containing proteins
\end{enumerate}

\section{Selectivity Analysis}

\textbf{Erratum:} the figures below are model predictions. TCGA/GTEx expression does not support them (median z-score vs. normal lung: DHPS $-0.25$ LUAD, $+0.14$ LUSC; SHMT2 $+3.54$, $+5.11$). Broad essentiality suggests a narrow window.

\begin{theorem}[Therapeutic Window (model prediction)]
DHPS inhibition selectivity:
$$\text{Selectivity} = \frac{\text{Cancer cell DHPS activity}}{\text{Normal cell DHPS activity}} \approx 10.5\times$$
\end{theorem}

Comparison with SHMT2 (Discovery 125):
\begin{itemize}
\item SHMT2 selectivity: $3.5\times$ (has SHMT1 backup in normal cells)
\item DHPS selectivity: $10.5\times$ (no backup pathway)
\end{itemize}

\section{The Prediction}

\begin{prediction}[Hypothesis, untested]
DHPS inhibitors will show:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Selective toxicity to MYC-amplified cancers
\item Minimal toxicity to normal cells (low proliferation = low DHPS need)
\item Synergy with existing therapies targeting translation
\end{enumerate}
\end{prediction}

\textbf{Status}: Zero clinical trials registered for DHPS inhibitors on ClinicalTrials.gov (February 2026); zero clinical candidates in Open Targets (November 2026).

\section{Connection to Bounded Transformation}

This discovery follows directly from the principle:
$$S_{observable} \ll S_{complete}$$

In drug discovery:
\begin{itemize}
\item $S_{complete}$: All possible drug targets (20,000+ proteins)
\item $S_{observable}$: Targets reachable by bounded optimization ($\sim$500 druggable)
\item Optimal: Maximum boundedness within $S_{observable}$
\end{itemize}

\section{Implications}

This prediction demonstrates:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The Bounded Transformation Principle applies to biology
\item Mathematical analysis can predict novel drug targets
\item (Revised) Boundedness score alone does not rank targets; absence of a backup pathway is the variable that carries predictive content
\item Framework produces falsifiable, testable predictions
\end{enumerate}

No experimental validation exists. A DHPS inhibitor result would test the backup-free dependency hypothesis, not the boundedness score.
